## 1. What Was Built

A "drop finder" picks the 3 most drop-like 32-bar windows in each track. At
genre inference time, the system tries 4 vectors (whole-track + 3 sections)
and keeps whichever gets the most kNN agreement. This is the "best-of-3"
strategy — the whole-track is always a candidate, so sections can only win
by beating it, but the selection mechanism has a bias toward high-seed-count
families (see §4.3).

### Architecture

```
downbeats → findDropCandidates(3) → 3 time ranges (32-bar windows)
                                     ↓
audio + time range → Python/ONNX → effnet → 1280-d normalized embedding
                                     ↓
section_embeddings table (video_id, model, vec_json, section_start/end)
```

- **Drop finder** (`src/fulltags/analysis/drop-finder.ts`): Scores windows by
`posScore × 0.7 + densityScore × 0.3`. Position prefers 25-75% of the
track. Density rewards shorter bar intervals.
- **Table:** `section_embeddings`, compound PK `(video_id, model)`
- **Model tags:** `effnet-discogs-1280-section-1`, `-2`, `-3`
- **Inference:** `genre-run-infer.ts` tries all vectors, picks highest agreement

## 2. Results: What Holds Up



### 2.1 Sections help overall


| Strategy      | Accuracy  | Δ vs whole |
| ------------- | --------- | ---------- |
| whole-track   | 51.0%     | —          |
| **best-of-3** | **58.0%** | **+7.0pp** |


The gain repeated across two separate random samples (n=80: +3pp, n=100:
+7pp). Direction is consistent.

### 2.2 More candidates don't help


| N     | Accuracy  | Marginal   |
| ----- | --------- | ---------- |
| 1     | 56.0%     | —          |
| **3** | **58.0%** | **+2.0pp** |
| 5     | 57.0%     | -1.0pp     |
| 7     | 57.0%     | +0.0pp     |
| 9     | 55.0%     | -2.0pp     |


Extra windows land on intros/breakdowns that add noise. best-of-9 is
WORSE than best-of-1.

### 2.3 Per-family: drop-defined genres benefit most


| Family     | Whole | Bo3     | Δ         | n   |
| ---------- | ----- | ------- | --------- | --- |

| house      | 77%   | 73%     | -4pp      | 22  |
| hiphop     | 72%   | 72%     | 0pp       | 18  |
| techno     | 81%   | 81%     | 0pp       | 16  |

- Groove "+25pp" is 1 track out of 4

## 4. Known Problems



### 4.1 Drop finder is mostly position, not energy

In constant-tempo EDM (the majority of the library), every bar interval is
identical, so the density score contributes nothing. The finder effectively
picks windows in the middle of the track.

**Better alternatives (not yet tested):**

- RMS loudness from the waveform (actual energy)
- Rekordbox PSSI phrase labels (Drop/Chorus/Build — already parsed in
`anlz.ts`, `phraseDropRoll()` classifies them)
- Combine: PSSI phrases when available, loudness fallback



### 4.2 Statistics have bugs

The reported McNemar/Wilcoxon values have errors:

- McNemar χ²=2.77 should give p≈0.10, not 0.019 (the CDF implementation
may be wrong)
- Wilcoxon p=0.0000 on binary outcomes is suspect — Wilcoxon signed-rank
is the wrong test for right/wrong data (McNemar is correct)
- No multiple-comparison correction (4 strategies tested)

**Required fixes:** Exact McNemar test (count flipped tracks directly),
Holm correction across strategies, drop Wilcoxon.

### 4.3 "Sections can only help" is false

House dropped -4pp. The "pick highest agreement" mechanism biases toward
genres with many seeds (House: 838 seeds, Trance: 84). The top confusion
(EDM→House, 7×) fits this — section embeddings pushed borderline EDM tracks
into the House basin because House has 10× more seeds.

### 4.4 Three families score 0%

Pop (9 tracks), Bass (1 track), Trance (3 tracks) are never predicted
correctly under either method. This is:

- **Seed imbalance:** House 838, Pop 269, Trance 84, Bass ~75
- **Not reported:** The report gives overall accuracy but not macro-F1 or
per-class recall, which would surface this immediately



### 4.5 Model comparison is underpowered

50 tracks against 50 internal seeds across 8 families is near chance. The
report admits this but still concludes "none beat effnet", while MusiCNN
tied on kNN accuracy and won on house/hiphop. MAEST used mean-pool over
tokens (wrong — middle blocks + stacked tokens is the right approach).

## 6. Why Effnet "Won" (and why that might be misleading)

Effnet was trained on Discogs genre labels → its embedding space is
pre-arranged by genre → raw cosine kNN works out of the box.

Self-supervised models (MERT, MuQ, OMAR-RQ, MAEST) aren't arranged that way.
They're designed to be read through a trained classifier (linear probe).
**Grading them with raw kNN understates them.**

The MAEST paper shows best features come from middle blocks with stacked
output tokens. The bench used neither — it mean-pooled the last layer.

**The model comparison must be re-run with probing before any model
conclusions are final.** The shortlist is now three models (effnet, MAEST
fixed, MERT-v2-30s) — see [experiment-plan](experiment-plan-2026-10-01.md).

### MERT-v2-30s (2026) — the new challenger

632M parameters, 30s clips at 24 kHz. Tops GTZAN (91.7%) and MTG-Jamendo
genre (88.0%). Caveats: authors copied competitor scores from another paper;
GTZAN is small/old/rock-heavy; the MTG-Jamendo lead is narrower. Layer 19
for broad genre, layer 23 for fine, both through a trained classifier.
~40× bigger than effnet — embed once and cache.
