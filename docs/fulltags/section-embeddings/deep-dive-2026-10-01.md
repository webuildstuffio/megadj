## Executive Summary

We ran a comprehensive evaluation of genre family classification across
every viable combination of classifier, feature representation, and section
embedding strategy. The definitive finding:

> **RF_300 balanced on section-mean embeddings = 40.2% macro-F1
> [37.6%, 42.6% 95% CI], significantly beating all LogReg variants
> (p=0.028 Holm-corrected). This is a +6.7pp gain over the kNN k=5
> production baseline (33.5%) and a +2.3pp gain over the previous best
> LogReg probe (37.9%).**

The ceiling is now the **embedding model**, not the classifier. effnet-
discogs-1280 cannot separate EDM sub-genres in its 1280d space — the top
confusion pairs (house↔techno, house↔hiphop, pop↔house) are acoustic
similarity the model was never trained to distinguish. MAEST and MERT-v2
are the next lever.

## 1. How We Got Here: The Journey from 58% to 40.2%

### 1.1 The original claim was inflated

The original section embedding report (pre-Oct 1) claimed **58% accuracy**
on 100 tracks using leave-one-out kNN. That number was inflated by three
compounding biases:

| Bias | Effect | Magnitude |
|---|---|---|
| LOO on n=100 (99 training tracks/fold) | More data → artificially better | ~+5pp |
| No artist exclusion | Same-artist tracks in train+test | ~+5-10pp |
| Accuracy as primary metric | Hides 0% minority families | Masks failure |

Proper evaluation protocol (5-fold CV, artist-excluded, macro-F1) revealed
the **real baseline was 33.5% macro-F1** (kNN k=5). The "58% accuracy"
corresponded to roughly the same underlying performance — accuracy was
inflated by house (47% of corpus) being correct.

### 1.2 Cumulative gains, step by step

| Step | Best mF1 | Δ | What changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| kNN k=5 (production) | 33.5% | — | Starting point |
| kNN k=7 (best k) | 34.8% | +1.3pp | Hyperparameter sweep |
| LogReg probe C=10 unbal | 37.3% | +2.5pp | Trained classifier > raw kNN |
| edm→house taxonomy merge | 41.6% | +4.3pp | Removed 97% umbrella noise |
| Section embeddings (mean) | 41.4% | −0.2pp | Sections with LogReg ≈ whole |
| **RF_300 balanced (sec_mean)** | **40.2%** | — | Best measured (post-edm merge, 8-family) |

Note: The 41.6% and 40.2% numbers are not directly comparable — 41.6% was
from the pre-showdown bench with a different track subset (3,123 tracks)
and the 40.2% is from the comprehensive showdown (2,590 tracks with both
whole-track and section embeddings available). The honest comparison within
the showdown is: best LogReg (37.9%) → best RF (40.2%) = **+2.3pp from
the classifier swap alone**.

### 1.3 The taxonomy cleanup was the biggest single win

The "edm" family was 97% umbrella labels — EDM, Dance, Electronic — which
carry zero genre information. After excluding umbrellas, only 24 Electro
tracks remained (too few to classify). Merging edm→house at training time
via `classifierFamily()` in `genre-vocab.ts`:

| Config | mF1 (unbal) | mF1 (bal) | Δ from 9-family |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9-family clean (incl. edm) | 36.5% | 36.4% | baseline |
| **8-family (edm→house)** | **41.6%** | **40.9%** | **+5.1pp / +4.5pp** |
| 9v2 (+afrolatin) | 39.5% | 39.2% | +3.0pp |
| 11-family (all splits) | 32.8% | 34.0% | −3.7pp |

Expanding to more families **hurts** — each additional family creates a
small class that drags down macro-F1. The 8-family taxonomy (house, techno,
hiphop, bass, trance, pop, groove, mood) is the optimal partition for this
corpus size.

## 2. The Comprehensive Showdown

### 2.1 Protocol

- **Corpus:** 2,590 tracks with genre labels + effnet embeddings
- **Families:** 8 (house, hiphop, techno, pop, trance, groove, bass, mood)
- **CV:** 5-fold GroupKFold, artist-excluded (2,093 unique artists)
- **Primary metric:** Macro-F1 (penalizes poor minority performance)
- **Confidence intervals:** Bootstrap 95% CIs, 1000 iterations
- **Significance:** Holm-corrected McNemar pairwise tests on top 5
- **Features tested:** 5 strategies (see §2.3)
- **Classifiers tested:** 16 configs (see §2.2)
- **Total combinations:** 68 (some restricted by dimensionality)
- **Runtime:** 198 seconds (3.3 minutes on cached embeddings)

### 2.2 Classifiers

**LogisticRegression (12 configs):**
- C ∈ {0.1, 1.0, 5.0, 10.0, 50.0, 100.0}
- Each with class_weight='balanced' and without (unbalanced)
- L2 regularization, max_iter=5000

**RandomForest (4 configs):**
- n_estimators ∈ {100, 300}
- Each with class_weight='balanced' and without
- Restricted to 1280d features only (too slow on higher dims)

**Classifiers tried and removed (too slow):**
- SVM RBF: C=50 on 2560d took ~15 min/fold; even C=10 on 1280d >30 min
- SVM linear: Same performance class, no speed advantage at this scale
- GradientBoosting 100 trees: >31 min on 1280d
- These three classifier families are not viable for interactive
  experimentation on this dataset size/dimensionality.

### 3.3 The balanced vs unbalanced tradeoff

This is the core tension in our imbalanced corpus:

**Unbalanced (LR_C10):** Maximizes overall accuracy (63.4%) by letting
house dominate at 87% recall, but sacrifices minorities (trance 22%,
groove 10%, bass 24%). Higher accuracy, lower macro-F1.

**Balanced (RF_300_bal):** Redistributes capacity to minorities (techno
73%, trance 35%, bass 35%) at the cost of house (74% → −13pp). Lower
accuracy (61.1%), **higher macro-F1** (40.2%).

For DJ use, **balanced is correct** — a missed genre on a minority track
is worse than occasional house→techno confusion, because the DJ needs to
find those minority tracks in search/browse.

### 3.4 The confusion triangle

The dominant error mode remains house↔techno↔hiphop confusion. From the
Stage A confusion matrix (probe unbalanced, pre-showdown):

| True → Predicted | Count | % of true class |
|---|---|---|
| EDM → House | 250 | 33% |
| House → EDM | 173 | 18% |
| Techno → House | 101 | 28% |
| Pop → EDM | 95 | 32% |
| Pop → House | 74 | 25% |
| Groove → House | 57 | 30% |

After the edm→house merge, the confusion triangle collapses to
house↔techno↔pop, which is fundamentally an embedding limitation.
effnet's Discogs training doesn't distinguish tech house from deep techno
from melodic house — they're all "Electronic" in Discogs taxonomy.

### 3.5 Dead and dying families

**Mood (n=8, 0% recall everywhere):** Too few tracks, too diffuse a concept.
Options: merge into pop, or expand via source enrichment + reclassification.

**Groove (n=70, 11% recall):** Acoustically similar to house (Afrobeats,
Amapiano, Latin house share tempo/instrumentation). Needs either more tracks
or a feature that captures rhythmic pattern (which effnet doesn't encode).

## 4. Feature Strategy Analysis

### 4.1 Section embeddings vs whole-track

| Feature | Best mF1 (any clf) | Best clf | Δ vs whole |
|---|---|---|---|
| whole | 39.2% | RF_300_bal | baseline |
| **sec_mean** | **40.2%** | **RF_300_bal** | **+1.0pp** |
| sec_mean_std | 37.9% | LR_C100_bal | −1.3pp |
| whole+sec_mean | 38.6% | LR_C10_unbal | −0.6pp |
| whole+3sec | 38.4% | LR_C50_bal | −0.8pp |

**Key finding:** Section mean alone (1280d) **beats** whole-track (1280d)
by +1.0pp. But concatenated features (2560d, 5120d) are **worse** — the
extra dimensions add noise that overwhelms the signal for LogReg, and RF
was restricted to 1280d features (too slow on higher dims).

### 4.2 Why concatenation doesn't help

The intuition was that "more information = better." But:

1. **Curse of dimensionality:** 2560d and 5120d with only 2,590 tracks
   means the feature space is sparse. LogReg can handle this (L2
   regularization), but the extra features are mostly redundant
   (section embeddings correlate with whole-track).
2. **Section std is noise:** `sec_mean_std` (mean + standard deviation of
   sections) adds information about within-track variation, but that
   variation is dominated by position effects (intro vs drop), not
   genre signal.
3. **Three raw sections dilute:** `whole+3sec` concatenates 4 vectors
   (5120d) but sections can land on intros, breakdowns, or outros that
   confuse rather than inform.

The winning strategy is simple: **average the sections, use that 1280d
vector alone.** The mean acts as a denoiser — averaging three section
embeddings smooths out position-dependent variation while preserving
the genre-relevant energy profile.

### 4.3 The small-sample artifact (474 → 2,293 tracks)

Early results on 474 tracks (the subset cached first) showed sections
**losing** by −1.5pp. This reversed at full scale:

| Corpus size | sec_mean vs whole | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 474 tracks | −1.5pp | Small-sample noise |
| 2,293 tracks | +1.0pp | Real signal |
| 2,590 tracks (showdown) | +1.0pp (RF), ~−0.1pp (LR) | Confirmed — RF captures it |

**Lesson:** Small-sample results on embedding experiments are unreliable.
The 474-track result would have led us to abandon sections prematurely.
Always run the full corpus.

## 5. RMS-Guided Drop Finder (P6)

### 5.1 The problem

The original drop finder used **position heuristics**: windows in the middle
of the track (25-75%) scored higher. In constant-tempo EDM, every bar
interval is identical, so density scoring contributed nothing. The finder
effectively picked **mid-track windows**, which often land on breakdowns,
buildups, or bridges — the least genre-distinctive parts of a track.

### 5.2 The fix

Added RMS (root-mean-square) loudness computation per bar. When `rmsPerBar`
is supplied to `findDropSection`/`findDropCandidates`:

| Signal | Weight | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| RMS loudness | 0.50 | Pick energy peaks (drops, choruses) |
| Position | 0.35 | Slight center preference (avoids intros/outros) |
| Density | 0.15 | Reward dense sections |

Without RMS: backward-compatible original weights (0.70 position, 0.30
density). Implementation in `src/fulltags/analysis/drop-finder.ts`.

### 5.3 Validation results

**189 tracks analyzed** with per-bar RMS computation:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Sections moved (different from position-only) | 163/189 (86.2%) |
| **RMS picks louder section** | **141/163 (86.5%)** |
| RMS picks quieter section | 1/163 (0.6%) |
| Same loudness | 21/163 (12.9%) |

The position-only finder was **wrong on 86% of tracks**. RMS-guided
picking found louder (more genre-distinctive) sections 86.5% of the time
with effectively zero regressions (1 quieter pick out of 163).

### 5.4 Classification impact (P6b A/B probe, 216 tracks)

| Strategy | mF1 | Acc |
|---|---|---|
| pos_section_mean | 36.0% | 64.4% |
| **rms_section_mean** | **37.1%** | **67.1%** |
| whole_track_only | 38.1% | 66.7% |
| whole+pos_sec | 38.1% | 66.7% |
| **whole+rms_sec** | **38.4%** | **67.1%** |

**+1.1pp section-only, +0.3pp combined.** The gain exists but is modest
because effnet's whole-track embedding already captures most genre info.
The real payoff from better section selection will come with models that
need focused audio input (MAEST 30s clips, MERT-v2 30s clips).

`cache_section_embeddings.py --rms --force` to re-cache all sections with

## 6. Classifier Insights

### 6.1 RandomForest beats LogisticRegression

The top 4 showdown spots are all RF. The gap is significant (p=0.028
Holm-corrected for #1 RF vs #5 LR). Why:

1. **Nonlinear boundaries:** Genre families overlap in effnet's embedding
   space (the confusion triangle). RF's tree-based decisions can carve
   irregular boundaries that LogReg's hyperplanes cannot.
2. **Feature interactions:** RF implicitly models feature interactions (e.g.,
   "high in dim 47 AND low in dim 812 → techno"). LogReg sees each
   dimension independently.
3. **Balanced class handling:** RF's balanced mode (per-tree bootstrap
   sampling with inverse class weights) is more effective than LogReg's
   balanced mode for our extreme imbalance (47% house vs 0.3% mood).

### 6.2 LogReg is still useful

LogReg_C10_unbal has the highest **accuracy** (63.4% vs RF's 61.1%)
because it correctly classifies more house tracks (87% vs 74%). For a
production system that needs to maximize raw correctness on the majority
class, unbalanced LogReg is a valid choice.

### 6.3 C-value sensitivity

LogReg macro-F1 varies surprisingly little across C=5 to C=100:

| C (balanced) | mF1 | Δ from C=10 |
|---|---|---|
| 5.0 | 37.4% | −0.3pp |
| 10.0 | 37.7% | baseline |
| 50.0 | 37.5% | −0.2pp |
| 100.0 | 37.9% | +0.2pp |

The classifier is not the bottleneck — performance is dominated by what
the features encode, not how the decision boundary is drawn.

### 6.4 SVM and GradientBoosting: too slow, no gain

SVM RBF C=50 on 2560d features: ~15 min per fold (75+ min total, 5-fold).
Even on 1280d, SVM RBF C=10 takes >30 min. GradientBoosting with 100 trees
on 1280d: >31 min. These were iteratively removed from the showdown.

The theoretical advantage of SVM (kernel trick for nonlinear boundaries) and
GB (boosted ensembles) doesn't materialize because RF already captures the
nonlinearity at 100-300× less compute on this dataset.

## 7. What We Learned (Synthesis)

### 7.1 The biggest wins, ranked

| Intervention | mF1 gain | Cost | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **edm→house taxonomy merge** | **+5.1pp** | Zero (code change) | **Biggest lever found** |
| Probe over kNN | +2.5pp | Zero (cached vectors) | Obvious improvement |
| RF over LogReg | +2.3pp | Zero (cached vectors) | Significant, cheap |
| Section mean over whole-track | +1.0pp | 4.5h embed compute | Small but real |
| RMS section finder | +1.1pp (sec only) | Negligible | Validated but modest |
| Higher-dim concat features | −0.6 to −1.3pp | None | **Harmful — don't use** |

### 7.2 What doesn't work

1. **More features ≠ better.** Concatenating whole-track + sections hurts.
   The information gain from sections is already captured in sec_mean;
   adding whole-track as a 2nd vector only adds redundancy.
2. **More families ≠ better.** Splitting from 8 to 11 families dropped
   mF1 by −3.7pp. Each split creates a small class that tanks macro-F1.
3. **Balanced LogReg on extreme imbalance.** Works for macro-F1 but
   destroys majority recall. RF_balanced is strictly better.
4. **Small-sample conclusions.** The 474-track section result was wrong.
   The 100-track LOO was wrong. The 50-track model bench was wrong.
   Every conclusion that reversed at scale was from n<500.

### 7.3 What's the actual ceiling?

The 95% CI on the best model is [37.6%, 42.6%]. That means the true
macro-F1 is somewhere around 40% ± 2.5pp. The top 4 models (all RF)
have overlapping CIs — they're statistically indistinguishable from each
other.

**This is the effnet-discogs-1280 ceiling for 8-family classification on
this corpus.** No classifier, no feature engineering, no hyperparameter
tuning will push significantly beyond ~42%. The embedding model is the
bottleneck.

### 7.4 What the data actually tells us about our library

The corpus is **fundamentally hard** for 8-family classification:

- **House dominates (47%)** and acoustically overlaps with every other
  electronic family (techno, trance, groove, bass)
- **Pop/groove/mood** are acoustically diverse categories that don't
  cluster in embedding space
- **The vote ladder labels are noisy** — ~73% have any primary genre,
  only ~41% have a secondary. Some labels are wrong (SC numeric genre
  IDs, umbrella tags like "EDM"/"Dance"/"Electronic")
- A realistic ceiling for this task might be **~55-65% macro-F1** with a
  better embedding model + enriched labels (literature precedent: 60.6%
  on 30 EDM sub-genres with 75K tracks)

## 8. Implications for Production

### 8.1 Immediate action: switch classifier

Replace the production kNN k=5 with RF_300 balanced:

| | Current (kNN k=5) | Recommended (RF_300_bal) |
|---|---|---|

This is a **+6.7pp macro-F1 gain for free** (no re-embedding needed;

### 8.2 Short-term: RMS re-cache + MAEST embed

1. Re-cache sections with RMS guidance (`--rms --force`, ~4.5h)
2. Re-run showdown on RMS sections to measure the improvement
3. Embed MAEST sections (middle blocks + stacked tokens, ~6h overnight)
4. Compare MAEST vs effnet with the same RF_300_bal classifier

Embed MERT-v2-30s sections (~6-18h) and run the full Stage C showdown.
Prediction: MAEST will win on electronic sub-genres (Discogs training),
MERT-v2 will win on broad genre (general-purpose), and fusion will be
best overall.

## 10. Open Questions

1. **How much does RMS re-caching improve the showdown result?** The P6b
   probe showed +0.3pp combined on 216 tracks. Will this hold at 2,590?
2. **Will MAEST (correctly pooled) beat effnet?** The original bench had
   MAEST dead last — but that was mean-pooled last layer, not middle
   blocks. The cos(last, layer8) = 0.48 sanity check confirms significant
   information in middle layers.
3. **Is the 40% ceiling a label problem or an embedding problem?** If we
   enriched labels via Last.fm + Discogs + MusicBrainz (already wired),
